Playtests said the kitchen felt mean. The mechanics didn't change. The feedback did. A field report on game-feel.

## What happened

Three separate playtest sessions ended with the same note: "the kitchen feels mean." The players weren't failing more than we expected. They were failing at the same rate. But something in how the failure was communicated made it feel unfair rather than fun.

## The diagnosis

We were telling players *what went wrong* before they had a chance to feel bad about it. The cat knocked over the coffee: error sound, red flash, score penalty — all at the same instant. There was no moment of "oh no" before the consequence.

In a game where chaos is the point, chaos should feel like comedy, not punishment. The fix was simple: add a beat. Let the cup wobble before it falls. Let the player see it coming and *almost* catch it.

## What changed

Nothing mechanical. The same things happen at the same rates. But each failure now has a wind-up. Players laugh instead of groaning. The kitchen still burns down — but now it burns down entertainingly.
