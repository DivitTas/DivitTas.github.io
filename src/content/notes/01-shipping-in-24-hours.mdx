What I learned scoping, cutting, and finishing Agneepath in a day. The honest version.

## The constraint

I gave myself 24 hours to build and ship a complete game. No cheating on scope ("I'll just finish this one thing after the timer"). Whatever was in at hour 23 was what shipped.

The result was Agneepath — a 2D action platformer with one level, one boss, and hand-tuned physics. It shipped. I'm still surprised.

## What the constraint does to your brain

The first six hours I wasted on architecture. I was building a game framework instead of a game. The constraint eventually forced me to stop — not by wisdom, but by running out of time.

Hours seven through twelve were the real work. With no time for abstraction, I wrote the simplest code that could possibly work. Functions that do one thing. Variables with obvious names. No generalization. It was the cleanest code I'd written in months.

## What got cut

- A second level (obvious)
- A dash ability (almost done — cut at hour 18)
- Music (made a sound effect loop work harder)
- The story (one line of text in the title screen: "go forward")

Every cut made the game better. The dash would have required rebalancing the boss. The second level would have been rushed. The music placeholder I had was wrong for the tone.

## The honest lesson

You know what you need earlier than you're willing to admit. The cuts I made at hour 18 were obvious at hour 6. The game that shipped was the game I should have scoped at the start.

The 24-hour constraint didn't teach me to work faster. It taught me to decide faster.
